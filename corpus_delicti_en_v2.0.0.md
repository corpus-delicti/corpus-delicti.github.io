
#### A narrative game for 2 to 4 players
A heinous crime has shaken the community. To solve it, the best investigators have been gathered, and you are one of them.

## TONE AND SETTING
Start by agreeing with all players on the kind of story you want to play. It could be a neo-noir tale on a moon of Saturn, a political plot in the Senate of ancient Rome, or a tragicomedy in a big-city neighborhood community.

It’s important that everyone is in tune not only with the setting but also with the tone of the story. If one player wants a dark thriller tone, like *True Detective*, and another prefers the comedy of *Knives Out*, there will likely be conflict during the game. Be generous and try to find common ground where everyone feels comfortable.

## CHARACTERS
Describe your character. Maybe you're a slick agent who likes vodka in his coffee, a little old lady writer with a brilliant mind, or an AI newly assigned to the homicide department. Write down their name, description, and any relevant details on a piece of paper.

When everyone at the table has introduced their characters, distribute twelve dice equally among the players. From now on, we will call the dice **Investigation Points** and each player’s set of dice will be called their **Pool**.

## THE LEAD INVESTIGATOR
Choose the first **Lead Investigator**. This role will rotate clockwise in each new scene. The lead will be in charge of facilitating the scene, in a role similar to that of a game master in traditional RPGs.

During most scenes described below, the investigators can collaborate by making suggestions, but the leader will have the final say on what happens. The first **Lead Investigator** will be the one who most recently solved a crime (or the most senior player if there’s no consensus).

From this point on, the story narration begins.

## THE CRIME SCENE
The first **Lead Investigator** will narrate the crime scene. They may detail how the victim was found, who they were, and how they were known. Write the victim’s name and the key details of the crime scene on a piece of paper and place it in a visible and accessible spot.

Before moving on to the next scene, the investigators must name the case: *The Butcher of Paris*, *The Black Widow*, or *Zodiac* are good examples.

Finally, pass leadership clockwise.

## THE EVEN SUSPECT
The next **Lead Investigator** must present the first suspect: the **Even Suspect**. They must explain how this suspect is implicated in the murder, their first name, who they are, and how they are known in the community. Write their name and relevant details on a visible piece of paper. Then, pass leadership to the player on the left.

## THE ODD SUSPECT
The next **Lead Investigator** must present the second suspect: the **Odd Suspect**. They must explain how this suspect is implicated in the murder, their first name, who they are, and how they are known in the community. Write their name and relevant details on a visible piece of paper. Then, pass leadership to the player on the left.

## THE INVESTIGATION
From this point, a series of **Investigation Scenes** will take place, with a new leader facilitating each one. The **Lead Investigator** will set the scene, decide who is involved, and choose the type of scene (interrogation, forensic analysis, field investigation, etc.). All players, including the leader, must describe the actions their characters take to gather clues.

When the scene is considered exhausted, players may use their **Investigation Points** to turn clues into solid leads to help solve the case. Each player secretly chooses any number of dice from their **Pool**, selecting the values based on which suspect they believe the clue points to: even numbers for the **Even Suspect**, odd numbers for the **Odd Suspect**. Players may choose not to invest dice, but the **Lead Investigator** must spend at least one **Investigation Point** in their scene.

When all players are satisfied with their dice and values, they reveal them at the same time. The **Lead Investigator** will ask each player what clues they want to turn into leads with their spent **Investigation Points**. One or more players can invest multiple **Investigation Points** into the same clue, indicating they want that lead to have more narrative weight. When asked, a player places each die on the piece of paper of the suspect it refers to, stating what clue it represents and why it should become a lead. The accumulated dice on each suspect are called the **Incriminating Pool**.

The **Lead Investigator** must close the scene by integrating the confirmed leads and discarded clues. Clues not backed by any player should be dismissed, with an explanation: a witness might be unreliable, organic evidence destroyed, or a partial print inconclusive. They must also narrate how other clues were confirmed: a blood drop matches a suspect’s DNA, a video places someone at the crime scene, or a witness agrees to testify.

This narration ends the current **Investigation Scene**. Pass leadership clockwise and start a new scene. If the new **Lead Investigator** has no dice left in their **Pool**, instead of hosting another **Investigation Scene**, they must run **Crime Resolution**. Proceed to the next section.

## SOLVING THE CRIME
In this scene, the **Lead Investigator** facilitates a discussion to establish the **Hypothesis of Events**: the storyline that will become the official version used by the justice system in the game world to judge the culprit.

All players present their theories on how the clues fit together. Unlike previous scenes, this one requires consensus among all players about the **Hypothesis of Events**. The lead only has a tiebreaker vote in the case of a complete division among participants.

Players who still have **Investigation Points** may use them here to support a theory by adding dice to existing leads (making them more relevant), confirming previously dismissed clues, or introducing new last-minute evidence.

Note that the **Hypothesis of Events** doesn’t need to point to the suspect with the most dice in the **Incriminating Pool**, though this can help guide the story. Leads with more dice should be significant but don’t have to be incriminating. A misleading clue that misdirected the team for a long time is still relevant, even if discarded in the final account.

The **Hypothesis of Events** must explain not only the leads that incriminate the accused but also why solid leads pointing to the other suspect are invalid, crafting an irrefutable story. Without this, it would be impossible to declare the accused guilty.

When all (or most) players are satisfied with the **Hypothesis of Events**, pass leadership clockwise and begin the trial scene.

## THE TRIAL
This scene tells how the suspect was apprehended and tried. The **Lead Investigator** must take the agreed **Hypothesis of Events** and narrate how the suspect was arrested and how the trial unfolded. Stop the story just before the verdict is read and proceed to a vote.

Now the players, including the lead, will take on the role of jurors. Each player writes their verdict in secret on a piece of paper. Players may spend 2 **Investigation Points** to gain an extra vote. These are the possible verdicts:

- Guilty: The accused is responsible for the crimes charged.
- Not Criminally Responsible: The accused committed the act but cannot be held legally responsible (e.g., due to mental illness).
- Acquitted: The accused is not legally responsible due to lack of evidence or justification (e.g., self-defense).
- Not Proven: The prosecution failed to prove the accused’s guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Innocent: It has been proven during the trial that the person did not commit the crime.
- Case Dismissed: The case is closed due to lack of evidence, procedural errors, statute of limitations, etc.

The **Lead Investigator** collects and reads the votes aloud. If 2/3 of the jurors agree on a verdict in the first round, that will be the final verdict. If not, the jurors may discuss the evidence and **Hypothesis of Events**, holding as many votes as needed. In the second vote, an absolute majority (over half) is required. From the third vote on, a simple majority (more votes than any other option) is enough.

## EPILOGUE
The final **Lead Investigator** will facilitate an epilogue scene set at some point after the crime is resolved. It could be a deathbed confession, a newspaper letter, or a TV interview. In it, the real culprit describes the crime and their motives. Whether they're in prison or elsewhere depends on a dice roll.

The **Lead Investigator** rolls the **Incriminating Pool** of each suspect and sums the results. The higher total reveals the real killer. If there's a tie, this will remain one of those cases where the truth is never known.
